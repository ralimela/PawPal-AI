# Cat Care Guide

General guidance only, not veterinary advice. Needs vary by age and health, so your vet knows your cat best.

## Cat Life Stages

A cat is usually a kitten until about 1 year old. Adult cats are roughly 1 to 10 years old. Many cats are considered senior around 10 years and older.

## Feeding Cats

- Kittens usually eat three to four small meals a day until about 6 months old, then move toward fewer, larger meals.
- Most adult cats do well with at least two meals a day at regular times. Many cats also do well with several small meals spread through the day.
- Senior cats may need a different food or smaller, more frequent meals. Ask your vet.
- Cats are obligate carnivores and need food made for cats. Dog food does not meet a cat's needs.
- Use the feeding guide on the cat food label as a starting point and ask your vet for the right amount for your cat.
- Fresh, clean water should always be available. Some cats drink more from a pet water fountain.
- When switching cat food, change gradually over about 7 to 10 days.
- A cat that stops eating for more than about a day should see a vet, because not eating can quickly become serious for cats.

## Play and Exercise for Cats

- Indoor cats need daily play to stay fit and avoid boredom. Two or more play sessions of about 10 to 15 minutes a day is a good routine.
- Wand toys, balls, and toys that move like prey work well. Let the cat "catch" the toy at the end of a session.
- Scratching posts and high places to climb or perch are part of a healthy cat environment.
- Kittens have lots of energy and usually want several short play sessions a day. Senior cats still enjoy gentle play.

## Litter Box Care

- Scoop the litter box at least once a day.
- Empty, wash, and refill the whole box regularly, about every 1 to 2 weeks for clumping litter.
- A common rule is one litter box per cat, plus one extra, placed in quiet, easy-to-reach spots.
- Changes in litter box habits can be a sign of a health problem. A cat, especially a male cat, straining to pee with little or nothing coming out is an emergency.

## Grooming Cats

- Cats groom themselves, but brushing still helps. Short-haired cats usually need brushing about once a week, and long-haired cats several times a week or daily.
- Nails: trim nails about every 2 to 3 weeks.
- Teeth: brushing a cat's teeth with toothpaste made for cats helps prevent dental disease. Never use human toothpaste.
- Bathing: most cats rarely need a bath unless they get into something messy.

## Vet Visits for Cats

- Kittens need a series of vaccine visits, usually every 3 to 4 weeks until about 16 weeks old.
- Adult cats should have a wellness checkup at least once a year, including indoor cats.
- Senior cats often benefit from a checkup twice a year.
- Ask your vet about flea, tick, and heartworm prevention for your cat. Some cat products are given monthly. Never use a dog flea product on a cat, because some are toxic to cats.
