# Pet Safety Guide

General guidance only, not veterinary advice. In an emergency, contact your vet or an emergency animal hospital right away.

## Foods That Are Toxic to Pets

Some common human foods are dangerous for pets:
- Chocolate, especially dark chocolate and baking chocolate.
- Grapes and raisins, which can cause kidney failure in dogs.
- Onions, garlic, chives, and leeks, in any form, for both dogs and cats.
- Xylitol, a sweetener found in some sugar-free gum, candy, baked goods, and peanut butter. It is very dangerous for dogs.
- Alcohol, caffeine, and raw yeast dough.
- Macadamia nuts, for dogs.
- Cooked bones, which can splinter.

If your pet eats any of these, contact your vet or an animal poison control hotline right away.

## Plants That Are Dangerous to Pets

Lilies are extremely toxic to cats. Even a small amount of pollen, a leaf, or the water from a vase can cause kidney failure. Sago palms are very dangerous for dogs. Other common problem plants include azaleas, oleander, and tulip bulbs. Check whether a plant is safe before bringing it home.

## Household Dangers

Keep these out of reach:
- Human medicines, including pain relievers like ibuprofen, naproxen, and acetaminophen. Acetaminophen is especially dangerous for cats.
- Antifreeze, rodent poison, insect bait, and cleaning products.
- Some essential oils, which can be harmful to cats.
- String, thread, hair ties, and rubber bands, which cats may swallow.
- Batteries, coins, and small toys.

## Medication Safety

Never give your pet human medicine unless your vet tells you to. Give pet medicines exactly as prescribed, at the same times each day, and finish the full course even if your pet seems better. If you miss a dose, ask your vet what to do instead of doubling the next one. PawPal+ never suggests medication doses, but it can remind you to give medicine on the schedule your vet sets.

## Emergency Warning Signs

Go to a vet or emergency clinic right away if your pet has trouble breathing, collapses, has a seizure, is bleeding heavily, may have eaten something toxic, keeps vomiting, has a swollen and hard belly (a sign of bloat in dogs), cannot pee, or seems to be in severe pain. Keep your vet's phone number and the nearest emergency animal hospital saved where you can find them fast.

## Heat and Cold Safety

Never leave a pet in a parked car, even for a few minutes, because cars heat up very quickly. In hot weather, give plenty of water and shade, and walk dogs in the cooler morning or evening. If pavement is too hot to hold the back of your hand on for several seconds, it is too hot for paws. In cold weather, limit time outside for small, short-coated, young, and senior pets.
