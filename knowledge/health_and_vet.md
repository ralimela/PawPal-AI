# Health and Vet Care

General guidance only, not veterinary advice. Always follow your own vet's recommendations.

## Routine Vet Checkups

Regular checkups catch problems early, often before a pet shows signs of being sick. Most adult dogs and cats should see the vet at least once a year. Puppies, kittens, and senior pets usually need more frequent visits. Bring a list of questions and any changes you have noticed in eating, drinking, energy, weight, or bathroom habits.

## Vaccinations

Vaccines protect pets from serious diseases. Core vaccines for dogs usually include rabies, distemper, parvovirus, and adenovirus. Core vaccines for cats usually include rabies, panleukopenia, herpesvirus, and calicivirus. Puppies and kittens get a series of shots, then boosters on a schedule your vet sets. Other vaccines depend on lifestyle and location. Your vet decides which vaccines your pet needs and when.

## Flea, Tick, and Heartworm Prevention

Fleas, ticks, and heartworms can make pets very sick. Many vets recommend prevention all year, and many products are given once a month. Heartworm is spread by mosquitoes and is mainly a risk for dogs, though cats can get it too. Use only products made for your pet's species and weight, as your vet recommends. Some dog flea products are toxic to cats.

## Dental Health

Dental disease is very common in dogs and cats and can cause pain and other health problems. Brushing your pet's teeth with pet toothpaste, ideally every day, helps a lot. Dental chews may also help. Bad breath, red gums, drooling, or trouble eating can be signs of dental problems. Your vet may recommend a professional dental cleaning.

## Weight and Body Condition

Being overweight is a common health problem for dogs and cats and can shorten their lives. You should be able to feel your pet's ribs without pressing hard, and see a waist when looking from above. Measure food instead of guessing, limit treats, and keep up daily exercise. To help a pet lose weight safely, ask your vet for a plan before cutting food.

## Signs Your Pet Should See a Vet Soon

Call your vet for an appointment if you notice changes such as eating much less or much more, drinking a lot more water than usual, weight loss or gain, limping, coughing, lots of scratching, vomiting or diarrhea that keeps happening, or a big change in energy or behavior. Trust your instincts: you know your pet's normal. For signs of an emergency, see the safety guide.
