# Dog Care Guide

General guidance only, not veterinary advice. Needs vary by breed, size, age, and health, so your vet knows your dog best.

## Dog Life Stages

A dog is usually a puppy until about 1 year old, though large and giant breeds can keep growing until 18 to 24 months. Adult dogs are roughly 1 to 7 years old. Many dogs are considered senior around 7 years and older, and large breeds often reach senior age sooner than small breeds.

## Feeding Dogs

- Puppies usually eat three to four small meals a day, and most move to two meals a day somewhere between 6 months and 1 year old.
- Most adult dogs do well with two meals a day, about 8 to 12 hours apart, at the same times every day.
- Senior dogs usually stay on two meals a day but may need a different food or smaller portions. Ask your vet.
- Use the feeding guide on the dog food label as a starting point and ask your vet for the right amount for your dog's weight and activity level.
- Fresh, clean water should always be available.
- Treats should be a small part of the daily diet, commonly no more than about 10 percent of daily calories.
- When switching dog food, change gradually over about 7 to 10 days by mixing more of the new food into the old food each day.

## Walking and Exercise for Dogs

- Most dogs need exercise every day. Many do best with at least one walk a day, and often two.
- Many adult dogs need roughly 30 minutes to 2 hours of activity a day, depending on breed, energy level, and health. High-energy breeds usually need more.
- Puppies do better with short, frequent play sessions and short walks than with long, intense exercise.
- Senior dogs usually still enjoy daily walks, but shorter and gentler ones.
- In hot weather, walk in the cooler morning or evening. Hot pavement can burn paws, and heavy panting is a sign to stop and rest in the shade.
- Mental exercise counts too: puzzle toys, sniffing games, and short training sessions help keep dogs calm and happy.

## Bathroom Breaks for Dogs

Adult dogs usually need to go outside at least three to five times a day. Puppies need to go out much more often: after waking up, after eating or drinking, after playing, and before bed. Senior dogs may also need more frequent bathroom breaks. A regular schedule helps with house training.

## Grooming Dogs

- Brushing: short-coated dogs usually need brushing about once a week. Long or double coats often need brushing several times a week or daily to prevent mats.
- Bathing: many dogs only need a bath every 1 to 3 months, or when they get dirty. Use a shampoo made for dogs, not human shampoo.
- Nails: trim nails about every 3 to 4 weeks, or when you can hear them clicking on the floor.
- Teeth: brushing a dog's teeth daily is ideal, or at least several times a week. Use toothpaste made for dogs. Never use human toothpaste.
- Ears: check ears about once a week for redness, odor, or dirt.

## Vet Visits for Dogs

- Puppies need a series of vaccine visits, usually every 3 to 4 weeks until about 16 weeks old.
- Adult dogs should have a wellness checkup at least once a year.
- Senior dogs often benefit from a checkup twice a year.
- Many vets recommend year-round flea, tick, and heartworm prevention, often given once a month. Follow the product and schedule your vet recommends.
